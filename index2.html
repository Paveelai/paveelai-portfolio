Production Notes: The Virtual Pipeline & 24-Hour Cycle

Pan-Regional Art Direction

To serve Brazil, Mexico, Colombia, and regional South American distributors from a single asset suite, location scouting had to be strategic. Workshop spaces, street architecture, and coastal plates were selected to reflect a shared modern South American aesthetic instead of identifiable landmarks tied to one country.

Concept to Storyboard

Before opening 3D software, the entire film and stills suite was built on paper:

  • Mapped vehicle engineering highlights to real-world tasks for small business owners.
  • Built lighting and environment moodboards for client alignment.
  • Created shot-by-shot broadcast storyboards detailing camera angles, talent blocking, and payload capacity features.

Realism by Addition

Standard automotive retouching is subtractive: removing rigs, reflections, and road grit. A virtual CGI shoot is the exact opposite. Pure renders look sterile, so realism had to be added back manually:

  • Directed the CGI team to compress the tires against the asphalt so the vehicle sat with genuine road weight.
  • Integrated subtle atmospheric haze, authentic light spill onto the body panels, and motion blur to lock the CAD model to live-action talent plates.

The 24-Hour "Follow-the-Sun" Workflow

Production ran continuously across opposite sides of the world. The 3D team in the Americas rendered and shipped files at the end of their day. Our art team reviewed passes, marked up frames, and adjusted color grading during our daytime hours. Notes arrived in the Americas at their morning call, ready to implement with zero lost time.

Know Your Chevrolet How Things Work

Client: Chevrolet / General Motors
Date: Mar 2022 - Jan 2024
Platform: Official YouTube Series (46 Episodes)

Objective

Reclaim owner education from inconsistent third-party dealer clips. Build an official, branded video library explaining EV features, digital cockpit displays, and active safety systems.

Strategy

Replace expensive live shoots and dense manuals with a modular motion system. Instead of featuring a single car model, we designed an archetypal vehicle with Chevrolet DNA. This kept the content evergreen across model years and allowed GM to scale the framework to Buick, Cadillac, and GMC.

Execution

Directed 46 animated guides on Chevrolet's official channel. Recreated touchscreen menus and vehicle tech as clean vector graphics, turning complex engineering into fast, step-by-step visual answers.

Skills & Capabilities

Art Direction Motion Graphics Storyboarding Information Architecture EV Onboarding UI & UX Mapping Design Systems Content Strategy

Production Notes: A 46-Episode Scalable Motion System

The Problem

Drivers struggled with new EV and driver-assist tech, but their only options were 400-page paper manuals or sales-heavy dealer clips on YouTube. Chevrolet needed fast, official answers that kept customers connected to the brand.

Demystifying the EV Transition

A major focus was helping drivers shift to electric vehicles with confidence:

  • Visualized charging workflows, Regenerative Braking, and One-Pedal Driving in clear steps.
  • Mapped app-based battery management and route planning to ease range anxiety.

Model-Agnostic Design & Longevity

  • Illustrated an archetypal Chevrolet rather than a specific car nameplate.
  • Videos stay fresh and relevant even after individual models receive exterior facelifts.
  • The focus stays on how the technology works, not the sheet metal.

Enterprise Scale across General Motors

  • The system was so cost-effective that GM adopted the template across Buick, Cadillac, and GMC.
  • Cut live-action shoot costs across multiple divisions while maintaining consistent corporate quality.

Deliverables: 46 official instructional videos published on YouTube and the myChevrolet app; scalable motion graphics library built for rapid UI and firmware updates.
